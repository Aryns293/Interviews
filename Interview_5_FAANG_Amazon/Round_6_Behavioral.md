# Round 6 — Behavioral (STAR)
**Interview:** FAANG-Style / Amazon Bar Raiser Loop
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Style:** Faster pace. Less hand-holding. I will push back if your answer sounds rehearsed or too polished. I will ask for a different example if your first one feels canned.

> Every answer in this round gets evaluated against Amazon's Leadership Principles. You don't need to name-drop them — but your answer must DEMONSTRATE them, not just describe them.

---

## 90-Second Rapid STAR Sprint

> "I'm going to give you 3 prompts. 90 seconds each. Structure, finish, move on."

**Prompt 1 — Disagree:**
> "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a technical decision. 90 seconds."

*LP being evaluated:* **Disagree and Commit** — did you raise the disagreement constructively, and did you fully commit once the decision was made?

**Prompt 2 — Failure:**
> "Tell me about a time you failed. 90 seconds."

*LP being evaluated:* **Learn and Be Curious** — did you learn something specific, or is this just a repackaged success story?

**Prompt 3 — Incomplete information:**
> "Tell me about a time you made a decision without all the information you needed. 90 seconds."

*LP being evaluated:* **Bias for Action** — did you act or did you stall waiting for perfect clarity?

---

## Deep Dive — Rolewize Idempotency Decision

> "You designed the dual-layer Redis/Postgres idempotency at Rolewize. Was there a specific moment where you had to make a judgment call with no clear right answer? What did you decide, and looking back, would you make the same call?"

*LP being evaluated:* **Dive Deep** + **Ownership** — do you actually understand the implications of your own design decisions, or did you implement something without thinking it through?

---

## Contest Failure — Blind Spots

> "You've solved 1,500+ problems and hold competitive programming ratings. Tell me about a contest where you completely misjudged your initial approach and had to scrap it mid-contest. What did that teach you about your own blind spots as a solver?"

*LP being evaluated:* **Learn and Be Curious** + self-awareness

*What I'm looking for:* A real story about a specific problem type or pattern where you had a systematic bias or blind spot — not just "I made a mistake and fixed it." I want to know what you learned about HOW you think, not just THAT you can recover.

---

## Scoring Rubric
| Dimension | Strong | Weak |
|---|---|---|
| Pace | Finishes each answer in the time given | Runs over by 2+ minutes |
| LP coverage | Answer implicitly demonstrates the LP | Answer sounds like it was written to match a keyword |
| Specificity | References actual code, decisions, numbers | "We worked together and solved it" |
| Honesty | Admits when an outcome was bad | Every story ends perfectly |
| Self-awareness | Can identify their own thinking patterns and blind spots | "I don't really have blind spots" |
