# Round 7 — Hiring Manager / Bar Raiser
**Interview:** Cyber Security / Compliance-Heavy Company

---

## Resume Defense — The "Move Fast" vs "Stay Secure" Tension

> "Your projects (CodeSync, QueueFlow) were built rapidly for hackathons or portfolios. Our company operates under strict SOC2 and FedRAMP compliance. We move slower because every line of code is audited. Will you be frustrated here? How do you reconcile your hacker mentality with our compliance reality?"

*What I'm testing:* Maturity. Hackers get bored in compliance companies unless they understand the stakes.
*Strong answer:* "I move fast on prototypes because the stakes are zero. But in a compliance environment, the code isn't the product; the *trust* is the product. I enjoy the intellectual challenge of constraint. Building a fast queue is easy. Building a fast queue that mathematically guarantees auditability and isolation is a much harder, more interesting engineering puzzle."

---

## Judgment Scenario — The Inside Threat

> "You discover that a senior engineer on your team has hardcoded a backdoor password into an obscure microservice, supposedly 'for easier debugging in production'. They tell you not to worry about it and that they'll remove it next sprint. What do you do?"

*Expected:*
- This is a zero-tolerance scenario in security companies.
- Do not confront them aggressively, but do not ignore it.
- **Action:** You must report it immediately to the Security/Compliance officer or your manager. A known backdoor in production is a critical breach of compliance (e.g., SOC2), regardless of intent. You do not wait until the next sprint.
