# Round 7 — Hiring Manager / Bar Raiser
**Interview:** Early-Stage Seed Startup
**Note:** The CEO/CTO of a startup wants to know if you're going to drag them down with over-engineering or lift them up by shipping value. 

---

## Resume Defense — The Volume Question

> "You have 3 full projects, an internship, an 8.11 CGPA, and 1500+ CP problems — all in under two years. Be honest: how much of that was efficient work, and how much was just long hours? I want a real answer, not a humble-brag."

*What they are really asking:* Are you going to burn out? Are you a machine who just brute-forces time, or are you actually efficient?

*Strong answer:* "A lot of it was long hours, honestly. I don't pretend I did this working 4 hours a day. But I also got extremely efficient at not reinventing the wheel. For QueueFlow and gitlight, I heavily referenced the internal architecture of BullMQ and Git respectively, rather than trying to design from a blank slate. I reuse boilerplates. I ship rough UIs so I can focus on the backend logic."

---

## Judgment Scenario — The "No" Conversation

> "The founder wants a feature shipped in 2 days. You're confident it needs 2 weeks to do without breaking something else. What do you actually say in that conversation?"

**What fails:**
- "I just work the weekend and get it done in 2 days." (Leads to burnout and broken code; founders actually hate this because it creates tech debt they don't know about).
- "I say no, it takes 2 weeks." (Too rigid, inflexible).

**What succeeds (The Negotiator):**
- "I tell them: 'We can't do the whole spec in 2 days safely. But if we cut X, mock Y, and do Z manually for the first 10 users, I can give you a clickable flow in 2 days. The full automated version will take 2 weeks. Which one do you need more right now?'"
- Startups are about negotiating scope, not negotiating time.

---

## Close

**Q1:** "Why an early-stage startup? You have the resume for a FAANG company. Why take the risk, the pay cut, and the chaos?"

**Q2:** "What's a hat you're willing to wear that isn't 'Backend Engineer'? (e.g., writing docs, talking to angry users, setting up the AWS billing account)"

**Q3:** "Questions for me?"

*Strong questions:*
- "What's the absolute biggest existential risk to the company right now, and how can engineering help mitigate it?"
- "How do you decide when to throw away MVP code and rewrite it?"
