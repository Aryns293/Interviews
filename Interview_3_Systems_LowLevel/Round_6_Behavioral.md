# Round 6 — Behavioral (STAR)
**Interview:** Systems / Low-Level Deep-Dive Company

---

## Q1 — Conflict
> "Tell me about a technical disagreement with a mentor or senior engineer at Rolewize about an architecture choice. What was the disagreement, how did you handle it, and what was the outcome?"

---

## Q2 — Failure
> "You made the SIH 2025 internal round but didn't advance further. Walk me through what happened. What would you do differently if you entered again?"

---

## Q3 — Pressure
> "You maintained strong academics while running a CP grind and building 3 side projects simultaneously. When things got tight — exam season, internship deadlines, a contest — what gave first? Be honest."

---

## Q4 — Ambiguity
> "gitlight had no spec. Nobody told you which Git commands to implement, in what order, or what 'done' looked like. How did you make those decisions? How did you know when to stop?"

---

## Q5 — Initiative
> "Nobody assigned you 'go reimplement Git from scratch.' Why did you actually build it — what was the real motivation?"

*What I'm listening for:* Intrinsic motivation — curiosity, a specific thing you wanted to understand, a problem you wanted to solve. "For my portfolio" is a weak answer. "I wanted to understand how content-addressing actually works at the byte level" is a strong answer.

---

## Scoring Rubric
| Dimension | Strong | Weak |
|---|---|---|
| Depth | References specific technical decisions | Vague about what was actually built |
| Honesty | Admits gaps, failures, limitations | Everything was a success |
| Curiosity | Shows genuine interest in how things work | "I wanted to add it to my resume" |
| Systems thinking | Connects low-level decisions to high-level implications | Treats each problem in isolation |
