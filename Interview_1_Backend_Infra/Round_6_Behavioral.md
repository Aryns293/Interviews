# Round 6 — Behavioral (STAR)
**Interview:** Backend / Infra-Heavy Product Company
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Format:** Conversational. I'll ask you 5 questions. For each one, I want a structured answer: Situation → Task → Action → Result. I'll push back if your answer is vague or sounds rehearsed. Every answer must be anchored to something specific — Rolewize, QueueFlow, or another real experience.

> **Interviewer's mindset:** Technical rounds tell me if you *can* do the job. This round tells me if I *want to work with you*. I'm testing self-awareness, communication, and whether you'll fit a team that moves fast and owns outcomes.

---

## Question 1 — Conflict

> "Tell me about a time during your Rolewize internship when you disagreed with a code review comment or an architectural decision made by your senior. What happened, and how did you handle it?"

**What I'm listening for:**
- Do you take a principled position or just roll over?
- Do you communicate your disagreement constructively (data, tradeoffs) or emotionally?
- What was the outcome — did you change their mind, did they change yours, or did you escalate correctly?

**Instant red flags:**
- "I always agreed with my senior" — signals no independent thought
- "I just did what I was told" — signals no ownership
- Throwing the senior under the bus — signals poor team dynamics

**STAR signals that score high:**
- You raised a specific concern with a specific technical reason
- You proposed an alternative with a concrete tradeoff analysis
- You reached a resolution collaboratively, even if the final decision went against you, and you committed to it

---

## Question 2 — Failure

> "Tell me about a bug in QueueFlow or the Rolewize codebase that took significantly longer to fix than you expected. Walk me through the failure, what you tried, and what you ultimately learned."

**What I'm listening for:**
- Is this a real bug or a vague "I ran into challenges"?
- Did you have a systematic debugging process, or did you thrash randomly?
- What specifically changed in how you work as a result?

**Instant red flags:**
- "I don't really have a good failure story" — everyone has one
- A failure that's actually presented as a hidden success ("it was hard but I fixed it fast")
- A bug that turns out to be a typo — needs to be a real architectural or logical failure

---

## Question 3 — Pressure & Prioritization

> "You had a 2-month internship window to ship production-grade webhook infrastructure at Rolewize. How did you decide what to build first, what to cut, and how did you manage the pressure?"

**What I'm listening for:**
- Did you have a real prioritization framework (MVP first, nice-to-haves later)?
- How did you handle uncertainty — did you ask for help, or go silent and struggle?
- Did you ship something real, or did you scope-creep into the deadline?

---

## Question 4 — Ambiguity & Judgment

> "Were the requirements for the idempotency layer at Rolewize fully spec'd out for you, or did you have to make judgment calls? Walk me through one specific decision you made independently where there was no 'right answer' in the ticket."

**What I'm listening for:**
- Can you operate independently in an ambiguous environment?
- Do you document and communicate your decisions, or just implement quietly?
- Did you validate your decisions with your manager or did you go fully solo?

---

## Question 5 — Initiative & Teaching

> "You mentored 400+ students in C++ at RoundTable. What's the difference between a student who actually gets better and one who plateaus, in your experience? And what did you do differently for the ones who plateaued?"

**What I'm listening for:**
- Do you have a genuine framework for teaching and mentoring, or is this just a resume line?
- This tells me how you'll onboard junior colleagues in the future
- Empathy and communication are as important here as technical insight

---

## Scoring Rubric

| Dimension | What "Strong" Looks Like |
|---|---|
| Specificity | Every answer tied to a real event with concrete details |
| Self-awareness | Owns failures without defensiveness |
| Communication | Clear, structured, not rambling |
| Growth | Can articulate what changed as a result of each experience |
| Team fit | Shows collaboration without being a pushover |
